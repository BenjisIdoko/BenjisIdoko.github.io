01CNG-Connect Live My own product

Helping drivers find gas before they join the queue

Goal
Give drivers switching to compressed natural gas live station status, so they stop driving to empty stations.
Built
Live map, status reports, savings calculator, conversion centre directory, and a driver community. React, TypeScript, and Supabase.
Outcome
Live with drivers: a crowd-reported map of stations across 28 states, 451 accredited workshops, and automated tests behind every release.
Read case study → Open the live app ↗
  1. Ask for reports where the data is oldest

    Crowdsourced status goes stale fast. When a driver comes within 800 m of a station with an outdated status, the app asks them to report. Fresh reports arrive exactly where the map is least reliable.

  2. Rate-limit sign-in codes

    Every SMS code costs money, and a script could drain the balance in minutes. A 60-second cooldown per number fixes that. Sign-in stays cheap to run and resistant to abuse.

  3. Test the parts that must not break

    39 unit tests cover OTP services, the proximity alert engine, permissions, and phone validation. Changes ship with confidence under strict TypeScript checks.

04Unitora Live

Making property investment feel as safe as a bank

Goal
Let people own a share of property and follow how it performs.
Built
Design system, investor journey, and a WordPress and Bricks platform extended with custom PHP.
Outcome
Live with 120 investor accounts. One design system runs both the public site and the investor portal.
Read case study → useunitora.com ↗
  1. Unit types became their own data, not duplicated tabs

    Each property page repeated hardcoded tabs, so every change was manual. I gave each unit type its own record, rendered by a query loop. Adding a unit type is now data entry, and every property page stays consistent.

  2. One set of design tokens for the whole product

    Styles were spread across per-page CSS and hardcoded values. I moved everything into global theme styles on one token system. Marketing pages and the dashboard now change together.

  3. Launch with verified offline payments first

    Investors needed to invest from day one, so the first phase shipped offline payments with automatic references and an admin queue. Every investment is checked by a person before it shows as a holding.

05Nigerian Jollof NYC Live

Putting a caterer's real menu online

Goal
Make the online menu match the real one, and keep ordering simple on a phone.
Built
Storefront design, a restructured catalogue, a custom plugin, and a move to FluentCart.
Outcome
Every dish has accurate tray-size pricing, and customers pick protein and spice level without extra steps.
nigerianjollofnyc.com ↗
  1. Choices that don't change the price aren't variations

    As variations, protein and spice level would have meant up to 90 per dish. A small plugin adds them to the order instead. Products stay simple to manage, and every choice still reaches the kitchen.

  2. Match the catalogue to the printed menu

    Categories and pricing were rebuilt to mirror the menu, removing items that weren't on it. What customers see online is exactly what the kitchen sells.

06SkraibeThe Transforming Church, Ushafa Live

Proving volunteers are on site before they clock in

Attendance used to live in Google Sheets. Now 144 workers clock in by phone GPS, kiosk or QR pass across 38 teams, and every record lands in one system.

ttcushafa.vercel.app ↗
  1. A GPS buffer instead of a hard boundary

    Phone GPS drifts, so a strict 200 m boundary would reject staff who are on site. A base radius plus a configurable tolerance solves it. Staff clock in reliably, and the check still means something.

  2. Check permissions on the server, not the screen

    Hiding buttons doesn't stop someone calling the API. Every route checks the user's role and denies by default. Access rules hold even if the front end is bypassed.
